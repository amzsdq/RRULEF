## Finding
Observation #4 contradicts the prior single early-invocation sample as a general scheduler rule. For the 13:11 target, the observed invocation was 13:11:14.668, only 14.668 seconds late. Therefore the earlier -313.573 s sample should not be used to claim that the scheduler normally fires early; it was likely affected by schedule mutation while a run was already queued/in flight or by another scheduler semantic not yet isolated.

More importantly, this automation turn arrived around 14:11, roughly one hour after the 13:11 invocation. That is consistent with the underlying RRULE:FREQ=HOURLY recurrence. Rewriting DTSTART alone does not establish that every handoff will wake near-immediately, especially if the requested anchor is already at/past the effective scheduling boundary.

## Optimization implication
The current major utilization risk is scheduler semantics, not the 10-minute work cutoff. A controller that assumes `update DTSTART -> near-immediate next run` can accidentally leave nearly an hour of idle time. Keep the 10-minute cutoff unchanged until wake transport is characterized.

Next experiment should isolate one scheduling variable: write a DTSTART that is unambiguously in the near future at update time and then compare actual last_run_time. Do not infer idle_gap_sec from requested DTSTART; use actual invocation timestamps and capture end timestamps when a reliable clock is available.
